In simple words
We paid ₹3,396 to get one ₹499 order. The 3 orders brought in ₹1,897. So for every ₹1 spent, ₹0.19 came back. At ₹499, one bottle earns about ₹0 after product and shipping, so no ad cost can work.

Part 3

Where people drop off

2 minutes

Click to compare our best campaign with the last one.

Important
Checkout works. Nearly everyone who starts checkout pays. The loss happens on the product page, before people click “Buy Now”.

What the product page shows today

  • ₹499 next to a struck-out ₹899. ₹899 is the 2-pack price, so it looks like a fake discount.
  • The rating shows as “100.0” instead of stars.
  • No “cash on delivery” line near the Buy Now button.
  • “SPF 25” on the page, but the FAQ says “Is this a sunscreen? No”.
Show the maths
Clicked Buy Now % = clicks ÷ page visits Bought % = purchases ÷ page visits Last campaign: 4 ÷ 197 = 2.0% clicked · 3 ÷ 197 = 1.5% bought Ankit campaign: 198 ÷ 2,346 = 8.4% clicked · 83 ÷ 2,346 = 3.5% bought

All numbers from Meta Ads. “Clicked Buy Now” is Meta’s “add to cart” count.

Part 6

Every ad wore out within weeks

2 minutes

Cost per sale every two weeks, with the same budget. Click to switch.

Good news
A new ad (Oshine) started cheap again, with the same targeting. So the market did not say “no”. Each ad just found its keen buyers fast.
The lesson
We needed a new creator ad every few weeks. We did not make them.
Show the maths
Cost per sale (2 weeks) = spend in those 2 weeks ÷ Meta purchases in those 2 weeks Example: ₹20,898 ÷ 27 = ₹774

Oshine’s first block is only 5 days (15–19 Jun), because the ad started on 15 Jun.

Part 7

The price path

2 minutes

Cutting the price did not bring buyers back.

Click a price to see what happened.

In simple words
At about ₹1,100, 3 to 4 of every 100 visitors bought, when creator reels ran. The lower prices did not bring more buyers. They only made each order earn less. We changed the price about 11 times in 12 months, so we never learned what one price really does.
Show the maths
Earned per order (before ads) = price ÷ 1.18 − product (₹300–350) − shipping (₹100–150) ₹1,199 → 1,016 − 350 − 150 = ₹516 (high case: 1,016 − 300 − 100 = ₹616)

₹899 and ₹499 ran only with narrow Mumbai targeting and brand-made ads. So we cannot blame price alone.

Part 9

Repeat buyers: the core problem

2 minutes

1 of 109 real customers ordered a second time.

Each dot is one real customer who received a bottle.

What buyers told us

“used it only once”“bottle still not finished”“hard to press”“uneven spray”
Important
We planned a refill code and calls to 87 customers. Neither happened. So we do not know why people did not come back. This is the cheapest question to answer: we ask them.
In simple words
Healthy brands get 10 to 30 of every 100 customers back. We got about 1. Without repeat buyers, every sale must pay for its own ads, and ours never did.
Show the maths
Repeat rate = real customers with 2+ delivered orders ÷ real customers who received a bottle = 1 ÷ 109 = 0.9% Wider count = real customers who placed a second order ÷ 109 = 2 ÷ 109 = 1.8%

The second customer ordered again in August, but that order was not delivered. Removed from the count: founders, family, friends, test orders, the café deal, and re-orders after a failed delivery.

Part 11

How sure we are

1 minute
Proven by the data
  • No ads means no orders (Sep–Oct).
  • Each ad wears out within weeks.
  • About 1% of buyers reordered.
  • Checkout works; people leave before Buy Now.
  • Follower campaigns sold nothing.
Only suggested
  • How much the price changed sales.
  • That too many ideas hurt sales.
  • That Mumbai-only targeting made it worse.
  • Why buyers did not come back. Nobody asked them.

“Only suggested” means several things changed at the same time, so we cannot split their effects.
